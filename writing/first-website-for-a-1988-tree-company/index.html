What a tree company open since 1988 needed from its first website

By Zachary Spencer · November 12, 2026

Andy's PGM has done tree, landscape and grounds work in Sarasota since September 1988. It runs a crane and a bucket truck. Until 2026 it had no website of its own. I built its first one. This is what I built and why.

What a homeowner found before

When someone searched for the company, what came up was a directory listing that had not been touched since about 2016. It had hours and a phone number. It had no photos and no way to ask for an estimate. Decades of work in one town, and nothing online that showed any of it.

What an established company does not need

A newer company's website has to argue that the company can be trusted. Andy's PGM did not need the argument. Thirty-eight years in business makes it.

So the site had a simpler job: let the homeowner who just searched find the company, see the work, and reach it in one tap.

What I built

  • A page for every service. Tree removal, crane removal, trimming and pruning, palm trimming, stump grinding, storm cleanup, landscape and palm installation, sod, and grading. A separate page covers grounds maintenance for condo and commercial properties.
  • Only their own photos and videos. Every picture on the site is from an Andy's PGM job. There is not one stock photo.
  • A photo estimate form. A homeowner fills in a name, a phone number, the job address and what they need, and can attach a photo of the tree. The request goes to Andy.
  • The phone number first. On a phone, the number is on the first screen and in a bar at the bottom of every page.
  • Pages for the towns they cover. Sarasota, Bradenton and Charlotte County, plus seven plain-language articles on things homeowners ask, like when to trim before hurricane season.

Three decisions I would repeat

Real photos over polished ones. A homeowner deciding who to let work over their roof wants to see that crew and that crane, not a model in a clean hard hat. A phone picture from a real job does more than any stock image.

The call button before the form. The form is there for people who would rather not call. Everyone else should never be more than one tap from the number.

Plain language. The articles answer what homeowners actually ask, in the words they use, without tree-care jargon.

What a website cannot do

A site like this makes an established company easy to choose once someone finds it. It does not decide how many people find it, and it does not answer the phone when the crew is on a job. Those are separate problems, and they are the ones I work on through the Zenith Co. ads service. Andy's PGM is a website client, not an ads client.

The site is live at andyspgm.com. Open it on your phone, the way a homeowner in Sarasota would.
