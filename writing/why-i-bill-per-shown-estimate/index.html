Why I bill tree companies per shown estimate

By Zachary Spencer · November 12, 2026

Most agencies that run ads for tree companies charge a monthly retainer. I charge a flat fee for each estimate where the homeowner was actually there. This is why I set Zenith Co. up that way, and where the model has limits.

What each pricing model pays for

A typical retainer pays the agency for running the ads. The check is the same in a week with ten calls and a week with none. A typical shared lead site gets paid for a name and a phone number, and the same homeowner's request can go to several companies at once.

Neither one is paid for the thing a tree company owner cares about, which is standing in a yard with a homeowner who wants a price. I wanted my fee to depend on the same thing the owner's revenue depends on. The closest event to revenue that I can count, and that I can do something about, is the estimate that actually happens.

Where the idea came from

Before I ran ads, I built websites for tree companies. The first was for A Tree Surgeons Enterprise in Tampa. The part of that project that mattered most was not the design. It was a text that goes out when a call rings out, so a homeowner who reaches voicemail hears from the company in seconds instead of calling the next one on the list.

In a tree company the owner is often the most skilled person on the crew, so the owner is the one in the tree when the phone rings. The lead is rarely lost on the website. It is lost in the minutes after someone reaches out and nobody answers.

What counts as a shown estimate

A shown estimate means three things happened: a homeowner booked through the ads, the tree company arrived at the booked time, and the homeowner was there to walk the job. These do not bill:

  • Leads outside the service area
  • Requests for work the company does not do
  • Homeowners who do not show
  • Duplicate bookings
  • Estimates the company cancels

The owner marks each one shown or not shown. The ad spend goes on the owner's own card, in ad accounts in the business's name, so it never passes through me and I cannot mark it up. There is a one-time setup fee, no monthly retainer and no long-term contract.

What it costs me

Billing this way moves risk onto me. If a homeowner books and forgets, I did the work and earn nothing. That is the point. It forces me to do the unglamorous part: text and call every lead in the company's name, aiming for under a minute, then send a reminder before the visit. An agency on retainer can skip that and still get paid. I can't.

Where it does not fit

This service is newer than my website work, and I do not have a client case study with numbers to publish yet. When I do, it goes on the Zenith Co. work page, with the client's permission.

It is also not for every company. It needs an established company whose crews can take on more estimates, an ad budget of at least $50 a day, and an owner who keeps the open times on the calendar current. I take one tree company per service area, because I could not honestly run ads for two competitors in the same town.

How to judge the fee

Before a call with me or anyone else, work out what one shown estimate is worth to you: your average job value times your close rate on estimates. If your average job is $1,000 and you close a third of the estimates you walk, each one is worth about $330 in work. Those are example numbers, not a client's. A per-estimate fee well under your number leaves you money after the job. One close to it does not.
