Why I put a photo estimate form on tree service websites

By Zachary Spencer · November 12, 2026

A standard contact form asks for a name, a phone number and a message. On the tree service sites I build, the form also asks for a photo of the tree. It is a small change, and it fits how owner-operated tree companies work.

The problem with driving to every estimate

King's Tree Service in Palm Bay is run by its two owners, Bill and Jenniffer, who have covered Brevard County for more than 25 years. They do the work themselves: removals, trimming, stump grinding and storm cleanup.

When the two people running the company are also the two people on the job, every hour spent driving out to look at a tree is an hour not spent cutting one.

What the form asks for

The form on kingstreecare.com asks for a picture of the tree, the address, and a sentence about the job. With that, an owner can size up a request from wherever they are, before deciding whether it is worth the drive.

It is easier for the homeowner too. Someone looking at a leaning oak usually has a phone in their hand already, and taking a picture is less work than describing a tree in a text box.

What it does not replace

A photo is not an estimate. Someone still has to walk the yard for most jobs. The form changes the first conversation, not the last one: the owner starts it already knowing roughly what they are looking at.

Where else I have used it

I built the same kind of form into the site for Andy's PGM in Sarasota, where the request goes to Andy with the job address and an optional photo.

On the first site I built, for A Tree Surgeons Enterprise in Tampa, the form is shorter. It asks for a name, a phone number and the service needed, and it sits on the first screen so a homeowner can ask without calling.

The form is half of it

A form only helps if homeowners reach the page. For King's I also set up and run the Google Ads that bring homeowners in Brevard County to the site. The ad account is in their business's name.

I do not publish their lead or revenue numbers, because I cannot separate what the ads did from the season, the weather and the referrals that would have come anyway. The site is live, so you can open it on your phone and try the form the way a homeowner in Palm Bay would.
